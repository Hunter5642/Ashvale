# Ashvale

A small medieval platformer built with MonoGame, grown out of the Ashvale title screen.

The volcano above Ashvale has woken, and rocks are raining down on the valley. A knight
climbs the ledges along the road to rescue the villagers stranded outside the castle,
taking cover under the ledges when the rocks come.

**3D object:** the spinning shield power-up that turns up after the second rescue of each
level. See [The 3D model](#the-3d-model).

## Running it

Requires the .NET 9 SDK. From the project folder:

1. `dotnet tool restore` (first time only, installs the MonoGame content builder)
2. `dotnet run`

## Controls

|Key|Action|
|---|---|
|**Left arrow** / **A**|Run left|
|**Right arrow** / **D**|Run right|
|**Space**|Jump|
|**Down arrow** / **S**|Drop through a ledge|
|**Left mouse button**|Press a menu button|
|**M**|Mute or unmute the sound|
|**Esc**|Exit|

## Menus

The title screen has four buttons:

- **Continue** picks up a saved game on the level it was on, with the same lives. It is
greyed out when there is no save.
- **New Game** starts over from the first level, replacing any save.
- **Settings** opens a menu with two more screens: **Controls**, which lists every key,
and **How to Play**, which explains the objective and how to win or lose.
- **Quit** closes the game.

Each level opens with a story screen, and **Begin** starts it. When a level ends,
**Next Level**, **Try Again** or **Play Again** carries on, and **Main Menu** goes back
to the title screen.

## Playing it

There are three levels: The Road to the Castle, The Foothills, and The Foot of the
Volcano. Each one opens with the volcano throwing a few rocks clear of its mouth, and
then the knight is free to move.

Rescue **5 villagers** to clear a level. There is only ever one villager out at a time:
reach them and the next one turns up somewhere else, always a good run away and up on a
ledge. The first villager of each level waits on the road.

Rocks fall from the sky the whole time, and each hit costs a life. The knight starts
with **3 lives** and earns one more for every level he clears, up to 5. Getting hit
starts the level over. Losing the last life ends the game.

The first level starts with 3 rocks falling, and each later level starts with one more.
Every villager you rescue angers the volcano: it rumbles, throws up a shower of embers,
and another rock joins the fall, up to 8.

After the second rescue of a level, a **shield power-up** turns up, spinning above a ledge,
with a blue mark on the map showing where it is. Grab it and the knight's own shield starts
to glow. The next rock that would have hit him smashes on the shield instead, and the glow goes
out. There is one power-up per try at a level.

## The tilemap

The road and the ledges are a tilemap (`Tilemap.cs`). Each level is a text file in
`Content/`:

```
tileset
16, 16
90, 15
0,0,0,0,0,...
...
1,1,2,1,1,...
```

The first line is the tileset, the second is the size of one tile in the tileset, and the
third is the size of the map in tiles. After that comes one line per row of the map, so the
file looks like the level. `0` is empty, `1` and `2` are the road, and `3`, `4` and `5`
are the left end, middle and right end of a ledge. The 16 pixel tiles are drawn 2.5 times
bigger, so the 90 x 15 map fills the 3600 x 600 world exactly.

Every tile that isn't empty is solid from above: the knight lands on it and so do the
rocks. You can jump up through a ledge from below, press **Down** or **S** to drop back
down through it, and walk underneath it, which is what makes ledges useful as cover. The
road can't be dropped through. The low ledges can be reached from the road, and the high
ones only from a low ledge.

The tileset and villager art are generated by `Tools/generate_tiles.py`.

## The 3D model

The shield power-up is a 3D model built out of triangles in code, the same way as the crate
in the lighting and cameras tutorial. It is drawn with a lit `BasicEffect`, spinning and
bobbing above a ledge.

|File|What it does|
|---|---|
|`ShieldModel.cs`|Builds the shield: blue with a gold rim and a raised gold cross. The blue and gold parts are separate vertex buffers so each gets its own color|
|`ShieldPickup.cs`|Places the power-up over a ledge, spins and bobs it, and draws it|
|`Graphics3D.cs`|Shared 3D setup: the projection, the lights, and getting the graphics device ready to draw in 3D|

The model's three matrices are set up to match the 2D world:

- **World** scales, spins and moves the shield to its spot, in the same pixels as the sprites.
- **View** is the camera's `GetTransform()`, so the model scrolls, zooms and shakes with the
  world.
- **Projection** is orthographic, like SpriteBatch's, so one unit is one pixel.

SpriteBatch turns the depth buffer off, so `Graphics3D.Begin()` turns it back on before the
model is drawn. The power-up is drawn after the tilemap and before the knight.

## Saving

The game saves to `ashvale_save.json`, next to the game itself, using
`System.Text.Json` to serialize a `SaveData` object (`SaveData.cs`):

```json
{
  "Level": 1,
  "Lives": 4
}
```

It saves when a new game starts, when a level is cleared, and when the knight loses a
life. **Continue** reads it back in and starts the saved level over from its first
villager, with the lives the knight had. Winning or losing the game deletes the save.

## The world

Ashvale is three screens wide, and the camera scrolls to follow the knight. The map
across the top of the screen shows the whole world, the part you can see, where the
knight is (white) and where the villager is (gold). When the villager is off screen, a
gold arrow at the edge points the way.

The castle and volcano painting scrolls at half the speed of the road, so it looks far
away. Every other copy of the painting is mirrored, so the copies meet without a seam.

## Visual effects

|Effect|Where it is|How it works|
|---|---|---|
|Scrolling, zoom and screen shake|`Camera.cs`|Every layer is drawn with `SpriteBatch.Begin(transformMatrix: camera.GetTransform(...))`. The camera zooms in on the volcano at the start of a level and on the knight when a level ends, and shakes the screen for eruptions, landing rocks and hits|
|Parallax layers|`ParallaxBackground.cs`|The background batch uses a transform that scrolls at 0.5 times the world's speed|
|Pixel art layer|`Tilemap.cs`, `VillagerSprite.cs`|Drawn through the camera with `SamplerState.PointClamp`, so the pixel art stays sharp and no lines show between tiles|
|Volcano embers|`Particles/EmberParticleSystem.cs`|Sparks drawn with `BlendState.Additive` that fade from yellow to orange to red|
|Falling ash|`Particles/AshParticleSystem.cs`|Spinning, semi-transparent flakes, each tinted its own grey|
|Rock debris and dust|`Particles/DebrisParticleSystem.cs`, `Particles/DustParticleSystem.cs`|Rock chips tinted from a set of rock colors that tumble and settle on the road or a ledge, plus translucent clouds of dust|
|Explosion and sparkles|`Particles/ExplosionParticleSystem.cs`, `Particles/SparkleParticleSystem.cs`|Additive bursts when the knight is hit, when you rescue a villager, and as fireworks when you win|
|3D shield power-up|`ShieldModel.cs`, `Graphics3D.cs`|A lit 3D model drawn between the sprite batches, with the camera's transform as its view (see [The 3D model](#the-3d-model))|
|Glowing shield|`KnightSprite.cs`|A white copy of the shield arm drawn in blue with `BlendState.Additive`, as a pulsing halo behind the knight and a shine over the shield|

The particle engine (`Particle.cs`, `ParticleSystem.cs`, `RandomHelper.cs`) draws through
the camera, which it gets from `Game.Services`.

## Sound

Music plays on a loop the whole time, and sound effects play when:

- a button is clicked
- a volcano erupts at the start of a level
- the knight jumps
- a villager is rescued
- the knight picks up the shield power-up (the rescue sound, pitched up)
- the glowing shield blocks a rock (the hit sound, pitched up into a clang)
- a rock hits the knight (the music drops so the hit stands out)
- the last villager of a level is rescued and a fanfare plays

Press **M** at any time to mute or unmute everything.
